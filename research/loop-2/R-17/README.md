# R-17 — Human–AI paired productivity

**Research date:** 4 October 2026.  
**Admitted claims:** C-036 and C-037; dependencies C-033–C-035 and C-038–C-041 are reconciled without taking over their reviews.  
**Status:** substantial analytical literature review accepted after independent quality review; coordinated publication checkpoint. Research judgments do not establish a canonical thesis or select an author position.  
**Reading convention:** R17-S01–S11 identify the recoverable sources in §13. Relative links assume publication at `research/loop-2/R-17/README.md`.

## 1. Finding: continuity can be useful without making the pair an indivisible asset

The human–AI pair is a defensible unit of evaluation, but the evidence does not establish it as the universally superior unit of production. Existing research demonstrates several narrower phenomena: useful combinations of unlike judgments, benefits from some personalization designs, adaptation to a collaborator's behavior, and information value in observable work histories. Those phenomena provide mechanisms for the author's hypothesis. They do not jointly establish that a particular person's years-long relationship with a particular AI creates a durable, portable premium over well-equipped replacements.

The central distinction is between **the total value of accumulated continuity** and **a specifically relational advantage that depends on keeping this human and this counterpart together**. Continuity may be valuable because someone has collected relevant facts, developed reusable tools, improved an evaluation suite, practiced delegation, or learned when to distrust a system. These are real candidate contributions even when a fresh model can use the same records. A residual pair-specific advantage is a more demanding claim: after appropriate replacement and transfer comparisons, some productive capability still depends on the match. Neither question should be used to erase the other.

Three conclusions follow from the reviewed evidence and the comparison designs developed below:

1. **Assistance is not sufficient evidence of synergy.** A system can help its human operator while performing worse than an available autonomous alternative on the measured task. Conversely, a less accurate component can improve a combination if it contributes informative differences that the combination can exploit. The relevant benchmark and process have to be specified.
2. **Personalization is not sufficient evidence of accumulated relational capital.** A short prompt-based match, access to historical examples, and a long-lived mutually adapted relationship are different treatments. A gain from the first two does not prove the third, although either could contribute to its total value.
3. **Demonstration is not sufficient evidence of portable capability or a credential.** An executable work sample might reduce uncertainty about what a candidate's system can do. Its value depends on authenticity, future-task validity, reproducibility, permissions and the destination's needs. A hiring premium could reflect clearer information, access to better tools, or temporary signaling advantages rather than durable paired productivity.

The strongest research-ready formulation is therefore conditional: productive history can reside in the human, in reusable machine/system state, in shared routines, or in the surrounding organization, with interactions among them. Determining its location requires interventions that preserve some components while replacing others. The sources inspected here do not supply a longitudinal, controlled, cross-employer comparison of all those alternatives. This is an identified evidence gap in this review, not a claim that every possible study has been found or that the future asset cannot exist.

## 2. Exact corpus questions and the authorial boundary

The [historical ledger](../../../evidence/claims/CLAIM-LEDGER.md) states:

- **C-036:** “The productive asset may be a human–AI relationship, an independent machine actor, or a firm-owned system depending on setting.”
- **C-037:** “A machine counterpart’s accumulated relationship with a human could form relational capital and an executable résumé.”

The controlling authored passage is [current synthesis §12](../../../source/synthesis/2026-09-11-current-author-synthesis.md#12-a-competing-unit-of-analysis-the-humanai-pair). It imagines a counterpart that has learned a person's standards, intentions, characteristic failures and ways of working, and a professional who can demonstrate the coupled system's repeated output. Crucially, the passage leaves the relative importance of the independent actor and the pair open. It does not authorize a research worker to choose a universal winner.

The passage also contains a stronger comparative possibility: people who compound a productive system over years may differ from people who repeatedly start with a generic model. That comparison bundles several investments. A good review must investigate the bundle while also asking which investments cause the difference. It must not treat an impressive established system versus an intentionally context-starved novice setup as an estimate of relationship specificity.

[R-15](../R-15/README.md#1-result-the-asset-and-the-right-must-be-specified-separately) supplies four separate portability questions: technical reproducibility, legal permission, operational portability and economic portability. R-17 addresses principally the last two and the productivity evidence behind them. It does not allocate title to an adaptation, infer rights from possession, or equate the AI's behavioral role with legal personhood. The competing production arrangements can remain empirical possibilities without changing Luke's current, revisable decisions about human development or value capture.

## 3. Define the comparison before declaring a winner

### Units of production and units of control are different axes

“Human plus counterpart,” “independent actor” and “firm-owned system” are not mutually exclusive categories. A firm can own the software used in a tightly integrated human–AI pair. A worker can operate a largely autonomous agent. A firm system can contain several models, specialists, databases, routines and human escalation roles. Ownership describes control over specified assets; it does not by itself describe the production process.

For evaluation, a **standalone actor** should mean an operational configuration that completes the defined assignment without concurrent substantive intervention by the focal human. It need not mean a bare model with no tools, instructions or records. The cost of upstream human design, maintenance and occasional rescue remains relevant. Calling a system autonomous during a measured interval does not make its supporting organization disappear.

Likewise, a **fresh model** needs an explicit definition. It could mean a new inference session using the same base weights; a replacement with access to the established memory and tools; a new provider/model version; or an agent without any local information. These are different counterfactuals. A fresh session with the old system's authorized context may preserve almost all externally stored continuity. Comparing it with a persistent counterpart tests whether continuity needs a particular session or internal state; comparing a blank session tests the value of the whole history bundle.

A minimally interpretable comparison set is:

| Arrangement | What the comparison can establish | Main attribution risk |
|---|---|---|
| Human alone, with ordinary non-AI resources | Human baseline and retained competence | Artificially depriving the human of normal tools |
| Same human plus fresh generic model | Assistance available without local development | Model quality and interface differ from the incumbent |
| Same human plus fresh model with copied authorized memory, tools and instructions | Value of transferable system state | Export is incomplete or replacement receives inferior retrieval |
| Same human plus established counterpart | Total current performance of the experienced pair | Human practice, tool investment and history are bundled |
| Experienced counterpart with a matched new human | Machine/system portability and match specificity | New operator suffers temporary onboarding costs |
| Experienced actor operating alone | Current need for the focal human during execution | Hidden human preparation, correction and access remain |
| Best feasible fresh autonomous stack | Whether accumulated experience beats a contemporary substitute | Unequal development, compute or task information |
| Firm-integrated production system | Value of organizational complements and scale | Comparing different task scopes or responsibilities |

These are proposed experimental arms, not eight arrangements already evaluated in one study.

### Four different meanings of “better together”

Let an agreed higher-is-better outcome be measured for a human, an AI configuration and their combination. **Augmentation** asks whether the combination exceeds the human alone. **Best-component synergy** asks whether it exceeds the better of the two solo configurations. The second test is stricter. It does not require the combination's score to exceed the arithmetic sum of two scores, which would often be meaningless for bounded accuracy or quality scales.

**Economic complementarity** is different again. In a specified production model, increasing one input may increase the marginal productivity of another. Observing that access to AI raises worker output can be consistent with complementarity, substitution within some tasks, task reallocation, or several mechanisms together. One assistance experiment does not identify a production function's cross-effects or establish an economy-wide demand effect. R-03 and R-12 own those distinctions.

Finally, **economic preference for a configuration** depends on cost and constraints as well as its measured score. A pair that is slightly less accurate on a laboratory classification metric may be preferred because it completes authorized work, provides explanations to affected people, handles exceptions, or satisfies accountability requirements. Those benefits must be measured or defended explicitly. They should not be relabeled as observed accuracy synergy. Nor should a model's better benchmark score imply authority to make decisions that the institution has not delegated.

Output should consequently be evaluated as a vector: acceptable completed work, error severity, latency, human attention, compute and tool cost, rework, maintainability, privacy/security failures, and the human's later independent competence. Choosing a scalar value function is partly a decision about the intended use. Report the underlying outcomes so that different legitimate priorities do not get hidden inside one productivity number.

## 4. Assistance, complementarity and actual synergy

### The Vaccaro meta-analysis

Vaccaro, Almaatouq and Malone's preregistered review covers 74 papers, 106 experiments and 370 effect sizes from January 2020–June 2023, requiring human-only, AI-only and combined performance. Its three-level analysis finds augmentation, Hedges' g = 0.64 (95% CI 0.53–0.74), but negative best-component synergy, g = −0.23 (−0.39 to −0.07). Synergy heterogeneity is substantial, I² = 97.7%. Creation's estimate, 0.19 (−0.09 to 0.48), exceeds decision tasks' estimate but is not itself significantly positive. Relative solo performance moderates results; over 95% of systems leave final decisions with humans. Eligibility excludes tasks that cannot be performed alone. Topic/design selection limits representativeness; bias diagnostics flag augmentation, not synergy. These are standardized task-performance comparisons, not wage, cost or lifetime-capital estimates. [R17-S01, Results, Limitations and Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1)

This result blocks two opposite shortcuts. It prevents treating any gain over an unaided worker as evidence that adding that worker to an AI creates net task-performance value. It also prevents treating one pooled average as a law prohibiting useful combinations. The experiments in a synthesis are selected production designs, not an exhaustive search over all feasible organizations of work. A poor combination can reveal a coordination failure without proving that no better division of labor exists.

For R-17 the missing estimand is especially important. A comparison among solo and combined conditions does not automatically vary relationship history. Even decisive evidence of present synergy would leave unanswered whether it was created through years of pairing, obtained from a generic interface, or achievable by a replacement. Conversely, negative synergy under a short encounter would not by itself refute a learning mechanism that requires repeated experience. The burden is to test that mechanism rather than invoke unobserved future learning to explain away every unfavorable result.

### Differences must be usable, not merely present

Steyvers and colleagues combine 28,997 classifications from 145 people with CNN predictions on 4,800 distorted images using a Bayesian model. Fourfold out-of-sample comparisons include human–human, machine–machine and hybrid pairs. Some hybrids outperform homogeneous pairs even when one component is individually weaker; complementarity depends on relative performance and modeled correlation, and fails in some configurations. Class-specific errors and human confidence improve aggregation. The people supply judgments independently; the aggregation is statistical, not a longitudinal conversational partnership. Artificial image noise, selected architectures and fitted combination rules constrain generalization. [R17-S02, Results, Discussion, Materials and Methods](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10349250)

The analytical lesson is to distinguish **available complementary information** from **the interaction protocol's ability to exploit it**. A collaborator can contain useful information yet be ignored, misunderstood or followed in the wrong cases. The relevant comparison includes the routing or integration rule, not just the nominal capacities of the participants. A pair need not make the same judgment twice: one member may formulate the problem, another search a space, and a third mechanism validate a constraint.

[R-07's Bansal 2021 capsule](../R-07/README.md) supplies a separate controlled explanation/reliance comparison. It should not be confused with the 2019 update study below. [R-10](../R-10/README.md) owns the P&G and BCG experiments; [R-09](../R-09/README.md#ai-mediated-transfer-and-its-limits) owns the published customer-support evidence. Their interventions should be interpreted on their own denominators and task boundaries. None is a substitute for randomizing accumulated counterpart history. The chess evidence belongs to [R-05](../R-05/README.md); its bounded results are not a historical guarantee that a human-assisted configuration permanently beats the best contemporary autonomous one.

## 5. What relationship history might contain

### Human calibration can matter without machine-side learning

Bansal and colleagues' 2019 CAJA experiments let crowdworkers learn an adviser's error boundary, with a costly perfect fallback. An update changes accuracy from 80% to 85% halfway through 150 rounds. Incompatible new errors disrupt team performance; compatible updates fare better. The task controls ordinary problem-solving skill but is highly stylized; the stated Q1 protocol recruits 25 workers per condition and removes bottom-quartile performers. The no-update trajectory after round 100 is extrapolated, not a fully observed 150-round control. Additional classification-dataset experiments concern retraining compatibility, not professional field outcomes. [R17-S03, Experiments, Q1–Q3 and Fig. 4](https://aiweb.cs.washington.edu/ai/pubs/bansal-aaai19.pdf)

This identifies a plausible source of historical value on the **human side**: an operator has learned a particular system's strengths and weaknesses. It also identifies a possible depreciation mechanism. Better average model capability can change where failures occur, invalidating an experienced operator's policy. A preserved chat history need not preserve calibrated cooperation after a model upgrade.

The book should therefore avoid locating all accumulated experience inside the machine. A practiced person may do well with a fresh model because the transferable asset is problem decomposition or evaluation skill. Alternatively, the person may initially perform poorly with a replacement because expectations need updating. A short replacement penalty is evidence of switching cost; demonstrating a durable pair-specific advantage additionally requires measuring the replacement's learning curve and the original pair's performance over a relevant horizon.

### Retrieval can personalize a model without a unique relationship

LaMP evaluates seven personalized language tasks using user-history profiles, with both held-out-user and time-based splits. Retrieval-augmented prompting and trained approaches improve task measures relative to nonpersonalized baselines, while retrieval method and the number of retrieved items matter. The benchmark uses classification/error and reference-overlap metrics, not completed occupational work or a live paired-productivity experiment. The paper identifies realism, pretraining leakage and generation-evaluation limitations; it does not establish privacy protection generally. [R17-S04, §§2–4, Tables 2–3 and Limitations](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.399.pdf)

Historical examples can thus be productive inputs without requiring the model to have personally participated in producing them. This is not a reason to call the resulting value illusory. Curating the profile may itself require judgment, investment and repeated correction. It is a reason to ask whether the economically relevant resource is the relationship, its transferable record, the human's ability to maintain that record, or their combination.

[R-14's persistence taxonomy](../R-14/README.md#external-state-can-be-productive-without-weight-learning) separates these mechanisms. Retrieval, stored preferences, working context, learned adapters and updates to base parameters must not be collapsed into “the AI knows me.” Their technical differences affect reproducibility, deletion, cost, failure modes and replacement. R-17 adds the causal question: which of those changes produces additional useful work for this human, and which is equally useful to another operator?

### Matching can work before a history exists

Ju and Aral randomly assign prompt-induced Big Five styles to AI collaborators in a 40-minute advertising task. The August 2026 v4 manuscript reports 1,258 human–AI participants, 7,266 ads and 1,168 independent raters; selected ads also run on X. AI style interacts with measured human traits in ad-quality outcomes. Human traits are not randomized. The system uses a fixed GPT-4o snapshot with shared task context; the intervention is expressly not memory. The manuscript is substantively accessible, while final PNAS access here exposes metadata, abstract and disclosures. The authors disclose a personalization-company interest. [R17-S05, manuscript Experiment Design, Results and Materials and Methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13979v4)

This is useful evidence against treating all human–AI pairings as interchangeable. It is simultaneously an alternative explanation for a supposed historical premium: a suitable collaborator style can be assigned at the outset. If an incumbent pair beats a generic replacement but not a replacement matched to the person's current needs, part of the advantage may be recoverable configuration rather than an irreproducible history.

A match need not mean flattering the person or maximizing perceived similarity. The testable object is a response pattern that improves the relevant work. A user's preferred style and a style that produces better decisions may diverge. Stable preferences can also change, so a system that enforces an old profile may impose a cost. These are hypotheses for evaluation, not effects quantified by the personality study.

### Mutual adaptation is more than obedience, but it is not automatically cumulative improvement

Nikolaidis, Hsu and Srinivasa model a robot inferring human adaptability and deciding whether to lead or accommodate. Online simulated table-carrying studies compare this with cross-training and fixed policies (n = 69). Separate repeated trials (n = 43) do not support the predicted progression from greater trust to greater adaptability. A two-task study (n = 58) finds limited persistence of adaptation patterns after the robot's belief is reset. The predefined policies, task rewards and online interface are important limits: this is neither a workplace LLM deployment nor evidence of years-long compounding. [R17-S06, §§6–9](https://personalrobotics.cs.washington.edu/publications/nikolaidis2017mutual.pdf)

A model of reciprocal behavior makes the paired hypothesis intelligible without anthropomorphizing it. The person learns what the system tends to do; the system changes actions in response to evidence about the person; the resulting pattern affects work. Yet accommodation can teach a person to wait for the machine to yield, and mutual predictability can preserve an inferior convention. “Both sides changed” is a process description. Whether the change improved capability, whose objective it served, and whether it transfers remain separate questions.

These studies should therefore be read together rather than as a ladder of interchangeable proof. Calibration, retrieval, initial matching and online adaptation offer different explanations for why an experienced pair might outperform a blank-start comparator. A convincing account of relational capital must identify their relative contribution and interactions, not merely attach the relationship label to their combined result.

## 6. Rapport, learning and the human-team analogy

Lee and colleagues' 2012 field study personalized a snack-delivery robot using previous encounters. Of 32 sign-ups, 21 participants remained; allocation was by hallway, not independent individual randomization. The four-month service gave participants roughly two months' use. Some rapport, cooperation and engagement measures improved, but satisfaction and willingness-to-pay differences were not significant. The system included an out-of-sight human operator selecting scripts and handling problems. The final camera-ready version, rather than an earlier draft, grounds this account. [R17-S07, §§2–5](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/2012/3/HRI12_Personalization_camready_final.pdf)

This distinguishes three outcomes that everyday discussion often blends: a system can feel more familiar, induce more cooperation, and produce more valuable work. None logically entails the others. Enjoyment can be a legitimate benefit in its own right, but the book's productivity argument requires an appropriate output measure. A service experience that includes hidden human support also warns against attributing the whole interaction to autonomous machine capability. The relevant lesson is an accounting and attribution question, not a dismissal of assisted service design.

A stronger relational analogy comes from research on human teams. Lewis, Lange and Gillis randomly preserve or reassign group membership across three weekly electronics-assembly sessions. Of 434 initial students, 300 complete in 100 groups. The results support some benefits for initial performance and abstract understanding after multiple tasks, but not the predicted immediate transfer after one task. An exploratory expertise-stability analysis helps explain heterogeneous transfer, including benefits in some reassembled groups. These are student groups and functionally related tasks, not AI pairs or employer moves. [R17-S08, §§4–6, Fig. 2 and Table 1](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/247824012_Transactive_Memory_Systems_Learning_and_Learning_Transfer)

The analogy fits where members learn who can do what, coordinate specialized contributions and build common routines. It breaks if the human-team result is used to assume that a model has a human-like memory, that a digital copy reproduces mutual expectations, or that an existing team necessarily transfers to unrelated work. It also breaks if a benefit that travels with an individual's specialization is counted as evidence that the original relationship must remain intact.

For C-037, “relational” is most useful as a claim about **contingent joint performance**, not as a synonym for all accumulated context. The same person and system may form a highly effective arrangement for one task family and a rigid arrangement for another. A transfer test should vary substantive demands, norms, collaborators and institutional access separately. Moving from one writing task to another in the same interface is not equivalent to moving a production system to a new employer with different data, responsibilities and standards.

## 7. Experience can accumulate liabilities as well as capability

Glickman and Sharot study 1,401 participants across experiments on emotional, perceptual and social judgments. Biased AI can amplify human bias through repeated interaction; conditions varying the actual source and its described identity distinguish output effects from beliefs about the adviser. An accurate algorithm can instead improve human judgments. The authors explicitly leave the longevity of these changes unresolved. The inspected published article is an experimental mechanism study, not an estimate of persistent assistants' occupational productivity or long-term psychological effects. [R17-S09, Results, Discussion and Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02077-2.pdf)

The paired-capital proposition must therefore admit negative accumulation. A history could preserve a false assumption, normalize a weak check, or make a person less likely to consult an independent source. Conversely, reliable correction could improve the user's judgment. Either possibility depends on the quality of feedback and on which behavior the system reinforces. Repetition alone does not determine the sign.

The resulting evaluation problem is dynamic. A pair can produce better immediate output while reducing the person's ability to detect its errors later. A pair can also spend more time now on explanation or verification and obtain benefits on later work. [R-08](../R-08/README.md) owns learning and retained-competence evidence; [R-07](../R-07/README.md) owns meaningful intervention and reliance. Their outcome distinctions are necessary controls on any claim that a pair is “compounding.”

Compounding itself deserves care. An increasing stock of useful records does not establish increasing returns to additional experience. A larger history may yield diminishing benefits, greater search cost, stale information or more contradictory instructions. The economic test is the stream of useful services net of development and maintenance costs, compared with feasible replacements. Growth in token count, number of saved memories, subjective attachment or elapsed tenure is not a measure of that stream.

## 8. The firm is an alternative location of continuity, not a null condition

An established firm can supply common data, standards, evaluation cases, shared tools, customer relationships, authorization, supervision and recovery capacity. A worker–counterpart pair may be valuable partly because it can access those complements. Removing them during an export changes the treatment even if every permitted conversation record survives.

The opposite possibility also matters. A portable pair could reduce the cost of onboarding, preserve a worker's effective routines, or allow productive activity outside a large organization. A firm might retain a capable copy while the worker leaves with another useful configuration. These outcomes are not logically inconsistent. Their coexistence would undermine a winner-takes-all account of where the asset lives, but would not settle bargaining or distribution.

[R-15's component inventory and portability analysis](../R-15/README.md#3-what-is-the-proposed-asset) explain why a file, a license, a permission and a productive relationship must be tracked separately. [R-09's departure evidence](../R-09/README.md#41-departure-studies-isolate-different-losses) supplies the comparison with losses of human, collaborative and organizational capacity. [R-05](../R-05/README.md) distinguishes value creation from durable capture. These are analytical constraints on R-17, not empirical evidence that retaining an AI copy replaces a departing expert.

A useful departure comparison would examine at least four post-move arrangements: the worker with the permitted portable bundle; a new worker using the incumbent firm's retained bundle; a fresh machine using the incumbent's data and tools; and the old experienced actor without its original human. Destination-side onboarding and firm-side adaptation should be observed rather than treated as zero. Whether each configuration is legally feasible must be settled before data or state are moved.

The relevant counterfactual is not always replacement by another human. The firm may automate an entire task, distribute it across several workers, buy an external service, or redesign it. A valuable pair can exist without being the least-cost configuration for every scale or purpose. Conversely, a firm-wide system's aggregate advantage does not prove that every employee's specific contribution is replaceable. The unit of analysis should follow the intervention and the question, with nested units retained when useful.

## 9. The executable résumé: work sample, portable system or market signal?

### Three propositions need separate evidence

An executable résumé can mean at least three things:

1. **A demonstration format:** a candidate can run a system on a task instead of only describing past accomplishments.
2. **A productive bundle:** the candidate can lawfully bring and operate the demonstrated configuration in the proposed job.
3. **A validated labor-market signal:** the demonstration adds reliable information about future job performance and changes matching, compensation or contracting in a beneficial way.

The first is technically easier than the second, and the second does not establish the third. An impressive demonstration may be a selected success, a model capability available to every applicant, a heavily prepared pipeline, or a service that depends on unavailable data. It can nevertheless be informative if the employer is hiring access to that very bundle. The correct question is what the recipient wants to learn: the person's unaided competence, the performance of the pair, the cost of a reproducible system, or the candidate's ability to improve it.

### Work histories can convey information, but information is not the same as capital

Pallais's online-labor-market experiment hires 952 randomly selected workers and varies the detail of public evaluations. Hiring and more informative evaluations improve subsequent employment outcomes. The design is an intent-to-hire intervention: some selected workers do not complete the job, and detailed comments are restricted to sufficiently high performers. Data-entry work, platform selection and the bounded follow-up limit transport to professional hiring. The study directly supports an information channel for documented performance, not the productivity or portability of AI counterparts. [R17-S10, §§I, III and V](https://hceconomics.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/pdf/events/Pallais_2014_AER_v104_n11_inefficient-hiring.pdf)

Wiles, Munyikwa and Horton supply a distinct alternative: nongenerative writing assistance can improve hiring through clearer résumés. The accessible March 2023 manuscript describes randomized assistance during 2021 platform registration, with separate allocation, approval and nonempty-profile samples; the 2025 published abstract confirms the qualitative result. Its intervention edits presentation rather than testing an executable assistant. Marketplace sponsorship and restricted platform data matter. Final full-text access was not recovered, so detailed manuscript design is not silently presented as the final published analysis. [R17-S11, manuscript §§2–3 and final publication record](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.04528)

Together these studies motivate a sharper question than “does the AI résumé get someone hired?” A market outcome can change because information becomes clearer, more credible, more available, or easier to imitate. Those mechanisms imply different welfare and persistence claims. An early applicant premium does not identify aggregate job creation, a permanent return to paired capital, or a gain for every participant in the hiring market.

### What would make the demonstration credible?

The following are proposed validation criteria, not an existing universal credential standard:

- **Authenticity and provenance:** identify the person, task, system configuration, model version, permissions, preparation and substantive interventions that produced the result. A generated explanation or activity log is not by itself proof of causal contribution; R-13 supplies the trace limits.
- **Out-of-sample validity:** use unfamiliar, job-relevant tasks selected independently of the candidate. Include failure, escalation and recovery, not only successful artifacts. Keep evaluation tasks out of development and memory stores.
- **Reproducibility and reliability:** repeat under realistic conditions, disclose stochastic variation, downtime and human attention. A deterministic replay of a prepared answer is different from completing a newly specified task.
- **Attribution appropriate to the job:** compare the candidate alone, a generic tool, the supplied bundle and a replacement operator when the intended inference requires it. There is no need to ban tools merely to measure a role that will permit them; there is a need to avoid mislabeling what was tested.
- **Operational and lawful portability:** show that the destination can actually run the configuration with authorized information, valid access and feasible maintenance. A prior employer's credentials or confidential records cannot be treated as transferable merely because they improve the demo.
- **Incremental predictive value:** test whether the demonstration predicts subsequent completed work beyond ordinary work samples, experience and interviews. Then separately test effects on hiring decisions, compensation, retention and distribution of opportunities.

The design should also avoid making costly infrastructure a hidden admission fee. If an assessment intends to measure capability rather than wealth or platform access, it can supply comparable resources and examine the incremental value of a candidate's permitted customization. If it intends to purchase a specific bundle, it should price that bundle transparently. These are alternative assessment purposes whose normative desirability remains for the author and relevant institutions to decide.

## 11. Discriminating comparison designs

No experiments were conducted for this review. These designs specify what evidence could change the judgments.

### 11.1 A longitudinal continuity trial with two estimands

Randomly assign comparable workers or teams to persistent continuity, a fresh-session policy, or a portable-record policy using the same model family and contemporaneous versions. Collect a baseline before substantial interaction. Allocate development time and record access consistently, while recognizing that a treatment may change how effectively that time is used. Track full intent-to-treat outcomes, attrition, nonuse and failures rather than selecting only successful long-term adopters.

First estimate **the total policy effect**: useful work over time with all pathways that continuity actually creates, including better documentation, tool design and human practice. Then conduct controlled snapshot comparisons to estimate **which stored or learned components carry the value**. Giving a fresh model the incumbent's curated records is appropriate for the second question, not a reason to discard the first.

Evaluate tasks sampled after development, with blind quality assessment and consequence-sensitive error measures. Separate speed from quality and both from cost. A history that reduces briefing time but raises inference cost can still be valuable; the choice depends on the resource constraint. Prespecify what counts as completed work and include correction, maintenance and outside help.

### 11.2 Crossed human–counterpart replacement

At planned milestones, evaluate the original human with the established counterpart, the original human with a matched replacement, a comparably skilled new human with the established counterpart, and the new human with the replacement. Retain a no-switch control and randomize switching where feasible. A further condition transfers authorized records and tools without original internal state; another preserves internal state while withholding selected external records, when technically and ethically feasible.

The resulting factorial interactions are evidence about match specificity under the tested transfer protocol. They do not automatically deliver a unique allocation of the surplus to human, machine and relationship: interactions and complementary components can make attribution depend on the chosen counterfactual. Report the actual contrasts rather than dividing the surplus by intuition.

Measure both immediate disruption and subsequent adaptation. A replacement that catches up quickly implies a different asset life from one that remains substantially worse after realistic onboarding. Human learning carries across conditions, so a simple crossover can be contaminated; parallel assignments, order randomization and explicit carryover analysis are preferable to assuming a washout erases expertise.

### 11.3 Match capability, information and tools without hiding their costs

Run two comparator families. A **mechanism comparison** holds base capability, context opportunity, retrieval quality, tool access, compute and task information as constant as practicable. A **deployment comparison** allows each arrangement its realistically obtainable configuration and prices the entire bundle. The first helps explain causes; the second informs a practical choice.

This avoids a common false dilemma. Equalizing tools may be necessary to isolate history, yet tool selection may be part of what an experienced pair learned to do. It should be counted in the total system result and identified as a mediator. Similarly, a new model upgrade may dominate an old experienced configuration in practice, even if experience still adds value within each version.

Include an experienced standalone actor and a strong fresh autonomous stack with adequate instructions and tools. If a human must define goals or authorize consequential actions, record that work consistently in all applicable arms. Do not grant the autonomous condition hidden expert rescue while counting every interruption against the pair, or make the pair's opponent a bare chatbot incapable of executing the task by construction.

### 11.4 Transfer in increasing order of institutional distance

Test new instances of familiar tasks; related tasks with different surface features; different task families; new collaborators; a changed organization; and a different employer or provider. These are distinct transfer distances, not interchangeable replications. Reassess performance after model updates and changes in standards, customers or data.

For an employer transfer, construct an authorized export inventory before testing. Distinguish failure to export bytes, inability to recreate the runtime, unavailable permissions, confidential information that cannot move, and lower performance despite a valid functioning transfer. Each has a different remedy and economic interpretation. Reinstating a prohibited complement is not a legitimate research fix.

Study the retained firm system as well as the departing worker. Measure both sides' transition costs and the value of continued maintenance. Compare a retained copy with a fresh model using the same permitted institutional records. A result on one side alone cannot establish the distributional effect of copying or departure rights.

### 11.5 Prospective validation of an executable résumé

With appropriate independent consent and governance, compare an ordinary application/work sample with the same evidence plus a standardized live-system demonstration. Evaluate downstream job performance on held-out tasks. Randomizing whether evaluators see the demonstration estimates its informational effect; randomizing access to a portable system after hiring estimates a different productive effect. Neither should be substituted for the other.

Record whether the demonstration changes whom the employer selects, not just average ratings of fixed candidates. Analyze false positives, false negatives, subgroup validity, applicant preparation burden and the opportunity cost of evaluation. Examine whether competitors can cheaply reproduce the signal, whether evaluators recalibrate after widespread adoption, and whether benefits to some applicants displace others. A credential's initial premium and its equilibrium value need not coincide.

## 12. Remaining gaps, synthesis gates and author questions

### Evidence gaps that survived the review

The reviewed corpus does not identify the lifetime return to keeping one human with one persistent counterpart. It does not estimate how much of that return would survive a frontier-model change, a clean export, a long absence, a replacement operator or an employer move. It does not establish whether observed historical gains are increasing, diminishing or reversed after maintenance and adverse-feedback costs.

The designs also leave important populations underrepresented. Students, crowdworkers, selected professional tasks, image judgments and short creative assignments cannot collectively stand in for every occupation. A comparison with a simulated robot or an offline retrieval benchmark is evidence about a mechanism, not a direct estimate for a professional AI chief of staff. No amount of relabeling those results turns minutes, weeks or a few months into years.

The gap is not simply a demand for longer observation. A long observational panel of successful enthusiasts can still confound skill, task selection, resources and effort with continuity. Conversely, a well-controlled short experiment can identify a narrow causal mechanism without establishing its durability. The required evidence combines longitudinal measurement with credible replacement, transfer and contemporary-alternative comparisons.

### Cross-program reconciliation

- **R-14:** use the persistence taxonomy and [memory-evaluation limits](../R-14/README.md#4-memory-evaluation-is-not-an-occupational-qualification). Its official product-memory documentation establishes described features; it does not supply a causal paired-productivity estimate. Its [experiential-capital tests](../R-14/README.md#what-a-test-of-experiential-machine-capital-would-require) align with the two estimands above.
- **R-15:** preserve the separation among reproduction, permission, operational transfer and economic value. A productive counterpart is not one indivisible legal object.
- **R-05:** an attributable productivity gain is not a durable rent or a bargaining outcome. Chess and competitive-capture capsules remain there.
- **R-08/R-09/R-10:** retained human learning, institutional knowledge and cross-domain work are relevant pathways. Their detailed workplace and training capsules remain with their source-owning reviews.
- **R-01/R-02/R-04:** compare useful completed work and lifecycle cost; a successful product demonstration is weaker than a controlled deployment evaluation. Do not count the same field experiment as independent confirmation in multiple programs.
- **R-07/R-13/R-16:** meaningful intervention, observable failure/recovery and training-to-work transfer constrain the value of every configuration. A personal history does not certify safe authority or make a generated trace truthful.

### Questions reserved for Luke

No empirical finding requires Luke to select the pair, the actor or the institution as the universal future winner. A more substantive author choice is what the book wants the executable-résumé idea to do: describe a possible demonstration practice, forecast a labor-market institution, or advocate a particular arrangement of portability and control. Each is permissible as a separate proposition, with a different evidentiary burden.

A second choice concerns evaluative priorities. The configuration maximizing immediate output need not maximize human development, autonomy, resilience or equitable access. Research can estimate trade-offs and expose false equivalences. It cannot silently choose their weights. The review therefore preserves both the possibility of valuable durable pairs and the possibility that capable standalone actors or firm systems absorb much of their productive role.

## 14. Review method, access limits and stopping condition

This is a targeted analytical literature review, not a preregistered exhaustive systematic review or a new meta-analysis. Searches combined human–AI synergy/complementarity, persistent assistant history, personalization, mutual adaptation, compatibility after model updates, transactive-memory transfer, and work-history/résumé signaling. Recent searches were included to avoid treating pre-generative-AI experiments as the entire current literature. Primary research, final public papers and recoverable author manuscripts were favored; abstract-only leads were not promoted to substantive evidence.

The selection deliberately contrasts controlled task evidence, model-based aggregation, offline benchmarks, longitudinal interaction studies, human-team analogies and labor-market experiments. They answer different questions and are not pooled numerically. Studies cited by another review are cross-referenced rather than recounted as new independent evidence. R-17 owns the substantive Vaccaro capsule; R-04/R-07 should point to it. Shared-source ownership is methodological coordination, not a reason to suppress contrary findings.

Several search leads about long-horizon collaboration were conceptual, self-selected case studies, attitude measures or insufficiently recoverable. They did not supply the missing controlled replacement/transfer estimate. The conclusion is consequently bounded to the sources actually examined. Finding no such estimate here is not evidence of a mathematical impossibility or an exhaustive proof of absence.

Public PDFs were read with text extraction; tables and sample descriptions were checked where load-bearing. Publication metadata were compared with accessed versions. The appendix records manuscript/final differences, the failed Lewis PDF link, publisher restrictions, and browser-check failures. No account, paid API, commercial database, human experiment, third-party contact or restricted dataset was used. No private author or assistant history was consulted or offered as corroboration. This review did not test a real person's persistent counterpart, execute a hiring evaluation or transfer workplace information.

The stopping condition for this program is an analytical account with exact claim outcomes, substantive evidence, counterevidence, documented access and discriminating tests. It is not empirical closure of C-037. The further author-facing conclusion remains: **evaluate the pair where joint history plausibly matters; keep the actor and institution as genuine alternatives; identify what survives replacement and transfer before calling the advantage durable relational capital.**
