# R-14 — Persistent cognition and capital formation

**Research date:** 4 October 2026.  
**Admitted claims:** C-030, C-033, C-034, C-035 and C-047.  
**Status:** Substantial analytical literature review accepted after independent quality review. Research judgments are not author adoption or a canonical chapter; remote durability is verified separately after publication.  
**Reading convention:** R14-S01–S20 identify the primary sources in the bibliography/access appendix. Relative links assume publication as `research/loop-2/R-14/README.md`.

## Finding

Experience can already change a machine system's subsequent performance in several technically different ways. Some operate through stored information or reusable programs; some change trainable parameters. Controlled research establishes both useful retention and important failures. It does not establish that an individually experienced general-purpose agent reliably becomes a broadly productive, transferable, appreciating asset through years of ordinary work.

The distinction matters in both directions. Calling a memory feature “learning” does not prove the stronger forecast in C-033. Conversely, denying its economic value because the foundation-model weights are unchanged would discard a potentially useful stock of software, records and procedures. The economically relevant object can be a system whose constituent model instances are replaceable. The authored synthesis already recognizes this institutional mechanism in §9 and deliberately proposes a different, more strongly embodied possibility in §10.

Three conclusions follow from the evidence reviewed here:

1. **Persistence is a property of specified state under specified retention and deployment rules.** A recurring name, a larger context window, a trust score and a saved checkpoint are not interchangeable evidence of learned capability.
2. **Durable improvement is conditional.** Learning new tasks, retaining old tasks, adapting to changed circumstances, transferring to new settings and preserving safety are separate outcomes. Success on one can conceal failure on another.
3. **“Experiential machine capital” remains a conditional economic concept.** To establish an asset rather than a promising metaphor, identify the increment in reusable productive services, its counterfactual, its full costs, its depreciation and its dependence on other people and systems. Copyability, legal permission, technical portability and economic transfer are distinct questions.

## 1. Exact corpus questions and interpretive boundary

The [historical ledger](../../../evidence/claims/CLAIM-LEDGER.md) preserves these propositions:

- **C-030:** “V4’s reality discriminator, skill routing and trust-based RLHF progression assert technical guarantees not established by the specifications.” R-14 addresses the learning/progression component. Truth discrimination, action gates and trace reliability belong principally to R-13.
- **C-033:** “Persistent machine actors might change materially through experience and acquire domain-specific productive capabilities.” This is a forecast, explicitly distinguished from retrieving notes or receiving more permission.
- **C-034:** “Experiential machine capital names potentially valuable learned histories that may be copyable, transferable, leased or forked.” The definition must not assume its proposed asset properties.
- **C-035:** “Wind tunnels could develop specific persistent actors through simulated adversity and professional post-training, beyond evaluating general models.” R-14 examines the persistence prerequisite; R-16 owns the fuller training, simulation, rare-event and transfer review.
- **C-047:** “The tacit moat could erode through cheaper observation; counterfactual absence, relocation under surveillance and consent are provisional defenses.” R-14 examines the passage from observed experience to reusable adaptation, not a metaphysical definition of tacitness.

The public [MKS v3 specification](../../../source/theory/05-mks-v3-master-specification.md), §§2.1 and 6.1, describes an identity/state object and a simulation environment. Neither is a demonstration that the system learns a persistent parameterized competence. The [v4 specification](../../../source/theory/06-mks-v4-trust-first-architecture.md), §§2.3 and 4.2, describes correction records, trust increments, skill selection and permission to delegate. It does not specify an optimizer, training objective, parameter-update procedure, retained adapter or regression evaluation. The label RLHF therefore cannot establish that its proposed progression performs reinforcement learning from human feedback. An implementation could add genuine learning, but the public specification does not show that it has.

The [current synthesis](../../../source/synthesis/2026-09-11-current-author-synthesis.md), §§9–11, is more careful: it distinguishes institutions that retain lessons outside model weights from hypothetical actors materially changed by their histories. This review preserves that distinction. It neither reconstructs private implementations nor turns author testimony about a public workflow into an independent technical experiment.

## 2. A persistence taxonomy that prevents equivocation

The following is this review's analytical vocabulary, informed by the mechanisms in the studies below. It classifies what changes, not whether a product markets itself as an agent or memory system.

| Mechanism | What changes | What can survive a fresh invocation | What must still be established |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retrieval from an external corpus | Available records, index and retrieval policy | Records and search configuration, if retained and accessible | Whether relevant, authorized, current evidence is retrieved and used correctly |
| Notes, summaries and structured state | Selected representations of prior interactions | Saved text, databases, tool state, checkpoints | Whether compression preserves the distinctions needed for later work |
| In-context adaptation | Activations and behavior conditional on examples, instructions or history | Usually only what is explicitly retained and supplied again | Transfer outside the supplied context; restart behavior |
| Inference-time search or revision | Candidate answers, search tree, intermediate computation | Nothing necessarily; selected artifacts may be saved | Whether later tasks benefit after the computation is gone |
| Test-time parameter adaptation | A parameterized memory, adapter or other selected weights | Only the parts retained rather than reset | Which parameters change, on what objective, for how long and across which boundaries |
| Supervised fine-tuning | Model or adapter parameters optimized against training examples | A saved compatible checkpoint | Generalization, retention, safety and whether behavior reflects new competence or task-specific elicitation |
| Reinforcement/preference post-training | A learned policy or related trainable components | The resulting saved parameter state | Reward validity, off-distribution performance and side effects; see R-16 |
| Continual learning | Repeated adaptation over a sequence or stream | Accumulated state subject to interference and retirement | New learning plus retention/appropriate updating across a stated horizon and resource budget |
| Authority progression | Permission, routing, scope of action or oversight | A governance record | Whether capability improved; promotion can change output simply by changing opportunity |

These categories can coexist. A system can learn a retrieval controller during training, then use that fixed controller to update a database during deployment. It can retain a LoRA adapter while also loading notes. “The model did not change” and “no learning occurred” are then different statements about different boundaries. Equally, the presence of gradient updates somewhere in a system does not establish a persistent change to the foundation model, much less useful occupational expertise.

**A minimal persistence claim should specify five things:** the state carrier; its update rule; the retention/reset boundary; the evidence that it changes future behavior; and the future task distribution on which that change is beneficial. Checkpoint persistence is distinct from service continuity: a file can survive while a provider, tool integration or indispensable collaborator disappears.

“Fine-tuning” describes an adaptation procedure; “continual learning” describes a sequential problem and a desired combination of outcomes. Periodically fine-tuning on only the latest examples can be a poor continual-learning method. Replaying old examples may be economically sensible even though the state is not a self-contained lifelong actor. And test-time learning need not be saved after the test.

## External state can be productive without weight learning

The most direct correction to a weights-only economic story comes from systems that keep their language model fixed while retaining useful work products.

**Reflexion** stores textual reflections from feedback rather than updating model weights. In the published NeurIPS 2023 experiment, its ALFWorld evaluation covers 134 environments across six task types, with up to 12 trials and memory truncated to the last three reflections. The heuristic-feedback version completes 130 environments. These are cumulative repeated-attempt results, not a one-shot success rate or an estimate of retention months later. The paper also studies reasoning and coding. Its terminology of verbal reinforcement learning describes a functional analogy; it does not make the textual buffer an RLHF weight update. This is evidence that retained feedback can improve bounded subsequent attempts. It is not a demonstration of an independently experienced model whose new competence survives removal of the buffer. [R14-S10, §§3–4.1]

**Voyager** supplies the stronger external-artifact comparison. With frozen GPT-4-0314 and GPT-3.5-turbo-0301 accessed through APIs, it accumulates executable Minecraft skills. Its transfer test resets inventory and world: four previously unseen tasks are tested with a 50-prompting-iteration cap, three attempts each. Voyager completes all four in all three attempts; transferring its library also improves the reimplemented AutoGPT baseline on some tasks. But the task remains Minecraft and relies on high-level Mineflayer control, rather than solving perception and motor control from pixels. Three attempts provide little precision about rare failures. This establishes a bounded reusable artifact and some cross-controller portability, not occupational generality or superiority over every fresh model. [R14-S11, §§2–3, Tables 1–2, Appendix B]

These two mechanisms should not be collapsed. A reflection can help revisit the same failed instance; a tested program can be reused in a different instance and called by another controller. Both may matter economically, but their evidential strength for transfer differs. The latter also challenges the assumption that all accumulated value must stay attached to the identity of one actor. Experience can produce a transferable tool that benefits a successor.

The reverse qualification is equally important. A growing library may become redundant, inconsistent or incompatible with a changed environment. A successful artifact at time one is an input to later production, not proof of later success. To attribute an improvement to memory, compare the same controller with and without the relevant artifact, control for extra attempts and compute, and evaluate new task instances. To attribute it to the particular actor, also give a fresh controller the same authorized artifacts.

Official product documentation supplies a different kind of evidence. OpenAI's **Memory in ChatGPT**, inspected on the research date, describes personalization using past chats, saved information and other available sources; it says memory does not retain every detail. It distinguishes remembered context from use of content to improve models. This documents a product's publicly described functions and controls, not individual online weight learning or an independent causal estimate of productivity. The page is mutable and availability varies. Its existence defeats the blanket statement that current systems have no persistence, while leaving the stronger actor-capital claim untouched. [R14-S16, Overview; Saved memories; Privacy and model training]

## 4. Memory evaluation is not an occupational qualification

Memory engineering has improved, but “remembering” comprises record creation, retrieval, correct interpretation, temporal updating and abstention. A single accuracy score conflates those stages unless the evaluation exposes them.

**LoCoMo's original ACL 2024 publication** uses LLM-generated conversations grounded in personas and event graphs, followed by human editing. Appendix B/Table 5 reports **10 conversations and 1,986 QA items**, averaging 27.2 sessions and 16,618.1 tokens per conversation. Its QA categories include temporal and adversarial questions; the original scoring uses normalized answer-token F1, not the later third-party LLM-judge practices sometimes associated with the name. Long-context and retrieval strategies help but do not eliminate the measured difficulties. This is a small synthetic, human-edited conversational benchmark, not ten longitudinal workplace deployments. Later comparison tables reporting 50 conversations or 7,512 questions must not silently replace this publication's denominator. [R14-S13, §§3–5, Appendix B/Table 5]

**LongMemEval, ICLR 2025**, contains 500 human-curated questions embedded in constructed user–assistant histories. Its S and M settings contain approximately 115,000 tokens and 1.5 million tokens respectively. It tests extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, updates and abstention. The commercial-system pilot is separate: only 97 questions with three-to-six-session histories. The principal QA judge is GPT-4o-2024-08-06; retrieval metrics are separately available. Its experiments show that better indexing and reading matter, and perfect retrieval does not guarantee the right answer. Those findings cannot be converted into a current ranking of commercial products: the tested systems and history construction are specific to the study. [R14-S14, §§3–5, Appendix A.4 and E.5]

A newer primary source makes the conceptual boundary especially clear. **LongMemEval-V2**, arXiv v1 dated 12 May 2026, has 451 curated questions about web-agent states, workflows, local failure modes and mistaken premises. Histories come from WebArena/WorkArena-family environments. In the main evaluation a memory controller gathers evidence for a fixed Qwen3.5-9B reader, with context capped at 200,000 tokens. AgentRunbook-C reaches 74.9%/70.1% on Small/Medium, with reported query latencies of 108.3/139.9 seconds. Despite its “experienced colleagues” framing, Appendix E expressly distinguishes this precollected-history QA test from online learning and end-to-end task success. The result is an informative accuracy/latency comparison of memory systems; it is not an experienced employee productivity estimate. [R14-S15, §§3–5, Table 2, Appendix E]

A separate positional experiment clarifies why simply retaining more text is insufficient. **Liu et al.'s Lost in the Middle** varies the location of relevant material in multi-document QA and synthetic key–value retrieval. Its NaturalQuestions-Open subset has 2,655 paragraph-answer queries. Several tested models perform worse when relevant evidence lies in the middle; adding retrieved material can improve retrieval coverage without proportionate answer improvement. This is controlled evidence about context use in the tested model generation, not a timeless bound on all later architectures. [R14-S12, §§2–5, Appendices D–E]

### What a benchmark score leaves unidentified

The review's inference from these designs is that at least four different contamination problems need separate controls:

- **Training contamination:** evaluation questions, answers or near-duplicates may have entered pretraining, fine-tuning, prompts or a tuning loop. Public availability is a reason to check, not proof that contamination occurred.
- **Retrieval leakage:** a system may retrieve an answer key or annotation accidentally included with the permitted history. Legitimately retrieving the historical fact that a question asks about is the intended task, not leakage.
- **Repeated-test adaptation:** a method tuned against a fixed test set may optimize that set's quirks. Repeated successful attempts on the same instance measure something different from generalization to an unseen instance.
- **State contamination:** stale, mistaken, adversarial or misattributed records may be promoted into apparently authoritative history. That can persist without any weight change.

**AgentPoison** demonstrates why the fourth category is substantive. The NeurIPS 2024 study changes retrieved memory/knowledge records without fine-tuning the victim model. It evaluates Agent-Driver, a ReAct/StrategyQA setup and EHRAgent under an attacker model with partial database write access and a known embedding model, also testing transfer. The EHRAgent memory is augmented to 700 successful experiences rather than its original four. Those assumptions matter: the study establishes vulnerability under specified access and query conditions, not an unconditional failure rate for every deployed memory system. Its distinct retrieval, target-action and end-outcome measures should not be collapsed into one headline attack percentage. [R14-S19, §§3.2, 4, Appendix A.1]

Accuracy also depends on the judge. An answer accepted as containing a relevant insight is not necessarily a fully correct procedure. Use deterministic checks where the target permits them; validate semantic graders on adversarial and partial answers; report human audit disagreements. These are proposed evaluation requirements, not allegations that the cited authors concealed invalid results.

Finally, **appropriate forgetting and catastrophic forgetting are different**. A superseded address, revoked instruction or changed workflow should cease to govern current action while historical provenance may remain useful. An evaluation that rewards indiscriminate retention can select for the wrong behavior. Timestamping, source authority, correction precedence and explicit uncertainty are therefore part of a memory system's productive specification.

## Parameter learning is real but stability is conditional

### The stability–plasticity problem is not a single settled contest

A model must change enough to learn, but not so much that it destroys useful earlier capabilities. Preserving every old response can itself be wrong after a genuine change in the world. This makes continual learning a problem of selecting what to retain and what to revise, under a distribution of future demands.

**Kirkpatrick et al.'s EWC** protects parameters estimated to matter to previous tasks through a weighted quadratic penalty. Its experiments include permuted MNIST and sequences drawn from ten Atari games. The Atari system also uses task-context inference, task-specific replay buffers and a small set of game-specific parameters; it is not memory-free consolidation. Performance improves over its sequential gradient baseline but falls short of separately trained game networks. The paper itself reports approximation and capacity limits. This is real evidence of retained parameterized competence within those designs, not a general theorem that forgetting is solved. [R14-S01, Results, Fig. 4, Discussion]

**Van de Ven, Tuytelaars and Tolias** show why protocol matters. In controlled Split-MNIST/Split-CIFAR-100 comparisons, task identity is supplied during training; whether it is supplied or must be inferred at test distinguishes task-, domain- and class-incremental settings. Parameter regularization performs much worse in class-incremental tests than in task-incremental ones, while replay is comparatively robust. Their analysis emphasizes transfer and resource efficiency rather than merely preventing forgetting by assigning a separate network to every labeled task. The study is both a taxonomy and an empirical comparison, with simplified classification streams rather than open-ended professional work. [R14-S02, Three continual learning scenarios; Empirical comparison; Discussion]

**GDumb** supplies an important adversarial baseline: greedily keep a bounded example set, then train a new model from it when evaluation is requested. Prabhu, Torr and Dokania compare ten continual-classification formulations across datasets including MNIST and CIFAR, generally averaging three seeds. This simple method often outperforms more elaborate algorithms, but loses substantially to some baselines in the twenty-task CIFAR-100 setting. The result is not that starting over is always optimal: rebuilding at inference can be unacceptable for latency-constrained work. It shows that an allegedly experienced model must be compared with what a fresh model can reconstruct from retained examples, with rebuilding costs included. Final accuracy alone can reward a method whose operational timing is unsuitable. [R14-S20, §§2–4, Tables 2–5]

Together these studies prevent two opposite overstatements: that neural networks cannot retain experience, and that one successful retention technique supplies a universal lifelong learner. Knowing which task one is in, access to old examples, fixed versus expanding capacity, and the right to retain those examples are substantive assumptions. A firm-specific actor operating amid ambiguous, overlapping responsibilities may face a harder selection problem than an experiment with declared task boundaries.

### Language-model successes deserve their full conditional force

**LAMOL** uses a language model both to solve tasks and to generate pseudo-examples for replay. Its ICLR 2020 experiments use the smallest pretrained GPT-2, nine epochs per task, five disparate language tasks including SQuAD, WikiSQL, SST, semantic-role labeling and WOZ dialogue, plus a separate text-classification comparison. In the five-task table, the task-specific-token variant with 20% pseudo-replay scores 74.3 against 76.6 for joint training and 51.5 for sequential fine-tuning. These are averages of different task metrics, not a common productivity scale. Generated rehearsal reduces dependence on keeping every original example, but it remains training work and depends on the replay generator's fidelity. [R14-S03, §§3–5, Tables 1 and 4]

**Continual-T0**, in the final EMNLP 2022 paper, adds eight language-generation tasks to T0, examining 3B and 11B models with 1% rehearsal from previous task datasets. The authors compare final outcomes with task-specific fine-tuning reference scores and retain evaluations of the original training and zero-shot tasks. Their reported aggregate retention is high; zero-rehearsal ablations are markedly worse. The reverse-order check uses the 3B model only. Crucially, their LAMOL comparator performs poorly in this generative-task setting, so an earlier method's success does not generalize automatically. This is evidence for sequential acquisition and some instruction composition, not indefinite retention, task-order invariance over every permutation or arbitrary workplace transfer. [R14-S04, §§3–5, Tables 2–5 and 7]

**Ibrahim et al., v4 (September 2024)** examine larger-scale continued pretraining: 405M models under English-to-English and English-to-German shifts, plus a roughly 10B model under the weaker shift. Learning-rate rewarming/redecay and replay can approach training-from-scratch on the union of datasets. Replay comparisons hold token compute constant by replacing some new tokens with old ones. The main shifted experiments concern two successive datasets, on the order of hundreds of billions of tokens; there are no multiple-seed replications, and the German train/validation split was not deduplicated. This is substantial counterevidence to categorical claims that useful retained updating is infeasible. It is evidence about large training phases, not cheap individual adaptation from ordinary daily interaction. [R14-S05, §§4–6, 8]

The apparent disagreement is therefore partly about the problem. A well-pretrained representation plus limited supervised change and rehearsal is different from unprotected updates, arbitrary drift, an unlimited stream, or a stream selected by an agent's own changing actions. Positive results should survive that specification rather than be discounted merely because they are bounded. Negative results should likewise not be inflated into a permanent human monopoly.

### Forgetting is not the only way experience can cease to compound

**Dohare et al., Nature 2024**, examine loss of plasticity: declining ability to learn new problems, rather than only loss of old answers. Continual ImageNet uses downsampled images in sequential binary tasks, with 1,200 training/200 test images per task, 250 epochs and reset output heads; major results average 30 runs. Standard methods deteriorate across long sequences, while continual backpropagation selectively reinitializes low-utility units. Other experiments include CIFAR-100 and reinforcement learning. The ImageNet reset exposes task-boundary information, and the study is not a frontier-LLM lifetime experiment. Its finite experiments support an important failure mechanism and remedy, not a proof of infinite stability or universal transformer incapacity. [R14-S06, main text, Figs. 1–4, Methods]

For the proposed capital concept, this is a different depreciation risk from erasing prior skill. An old system could retain a useful procedure yet become costly to retrain when its environment changes. Conversely, replacing some internal features might preserve economic adaptability while destroying a simple narrative of uninterrupted identity. “Same actor” needs an operational definition that permits maintenance, version changes and rollback without using identity as evidence of superior performance.

### Self-adaptation and test-time memory do not close the lifetime gap

**SEAL's final NeurIPS 2025 paper** learns a self-edit policy whose generated data is used for weight fine-tuning. Main Qwen2.5-7B no-context SQuAD accuracy is 47.0% versus 33.5% passage-only (200 passages, 974 questions). Its nested reward uses downstream references and roughly 30–45 seconds per edit. Those requirements are configuration-specific: Appendix B.10's GPT-4.1 rubric proxy reaches 45.6% versus 47.0%, with about five minutes versus six hours of RL training. In B.11, untrained rewrite/long-implication prompts achieve 49.4%/49.3%, exceeding the main 47.0%; RL further improves them to 55.6%/54.4%. Attribution therefore depends on the prompt baseline and reward. Continued-pretraining tests (200/2,067 documents) favor GPT-4.1-generated data slightly over SEAL. ARC uses Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, eleven training/eight evaluation tasks selected for solvability and five evaluation self-edits, not the full benchmark. Sequential updates still forget earlier material. This demonstrates parameter adaptation, not guaranteed cheap or durable occupational learning. [R14-S07, §§3–5, Tables 1–2, Appendix B.10–B.11]

**Titans v1** changes a neural contextual-memory module at test time, while separately named “persistent memory” parameters are fixed at inference. This terminology must not be mapped directly onto the book's persistent occupational actor. Its reported model scales are 170M–760M, trained on 15B or 30B FineWeb-Edu tokens; evaluations include language modeling, reasoning and long-context retrieval. The architecture demonstrates a way to learn a parameterized representation of incoming context. It does not report years of retained actor-specific development across deployments, or show that the changing memory is always saved across sessions. A weight update can function as a recurrent state update rather than an enduring professional qualification. [R14-S08, §§3.1–3.3, 4–5]

The central comparison is thus not “memory versus no memory.” It is **which information is stored where, which update mechanism extracts reusable structure, and which future outcomes improve at acceptable total cost**. External memory can preserve exact facts and provenance; distributed parameter changes may support generalization or efficient execution but complicate targeted correction. Neither mechanism dominates by definition. Hybrid systems can be sensible, yet their success must be attributed to the whole tested configuration.

## 6. Copying, separation, privacy and the location of capital

Digital state is potentially reproducible, but five different propositions are often hidden inside “copy the experienced agent”:

1. Copy the bytes of an accessible record, checkpoint or adapter.
2. Recreate a compatible runtime, tokenizer, dependencies and tool interfaces.
3. Obtain the necessary model access, operational permissions and legitimate data-use rights.
4. Preserve performance in a different organization, with different people and current facts.
5. Preserve an economically valuable advantage over the destination's best alternative.

The first does not establish the others. Even byte-identical checkpoints produce stochastic trajectories unless relevant randomness and environments are controlled. A history of permission grants cannot be exported as permission from a different principal. A specialist may be valuable precisely because its routines fit an environment that the buyer cannot reproduce.

**LoRA** makes one technical separation concrete. Hu et al.'s ICLR 2022 method freezes pretrained weights and learns low-rank update matrices. Their experiments span RoBERTa, DeBERTa, GPT-2 and GPT-3; task-specific matrices can be stored and switched without storing a separate full base model for each task. Its reported savings are relative to specified full-fine-tuning configurations, not a universal cost ratio. The adapter still requires a compatible base and architecture, and the method does not assign one clean parameter block to every person, fact or legal interest. Modular storage therefore enables some portability without proving semantic or confidential-information separability. [R14-S09, §§4–5]

Privacy is not a side issue added after valuing the asset. It can determine whether the proposed transfer is feasible at all. **Carlini et al., USENIX Security 2021**, demonstrate extraction of memorized training sequences from GPT-2. Of 1,800 selected candidates across attack configurations, more than 600 were confirmed verbatim training samples. These are selected-candidate results, not a probability that any arbitrary private fact will be disclosed. The model was trained on public-web data, so the study is not direct evidence of a particular workplace agent leaking employer secrets. It nevertheless defeats the assumption that parameters necessarily abstract away source information sufficiently to make unrestricted copying harmless. [R14-S17, §§3–6]

**TOFU v1** controls information origin with 200 fictitious author profiles, twenty QA pairs each, learned by Llama-2-7B and Phi-1.5. It tests removing 1%, 5% or 10% while retaining utility and compares with models never trained on the removed subset. The four examined baseline methods fail to deliver satisfactory forgetting without trade-offs across the tested metrics. Because the data are synthetic and the methods limited, this does not prove all selective unlearning impossible. It does show why suppression on a few questions, or removal of source files after training, cannot certify clean separation of confidential experience from general capability. [R14-S18, §§2–4, Appendix C]

The corresponding external-state issue is easier to inspect in some respects, but not trivial: deleting a record does not automatically remove its summaries, copies, derived code or downstream training effects. The relevant engineering question is a data-and-state lineage question, not simply whether a user interface offers “forget.” Security enforcement and malicious-record handling belong to [R-13](../R-13/README.md); they must not be inferred from retention alone.

The economic and legal analysis is deliberately shared with [R-15, §§3–4](../R-15/README.md#3-what-is-the-proposed-asset), which distinguishes control rights, technical reproducibility and economic portability. Its theories of firm-specific investment and complementary resources do not become theorems about a copyable neural checkpoint. If a newly trained adapter can be used by many people, copying may spread productive services while weakening scarcity rents. If its value depends on a private workflow, scarce feedback, authorized access or a trusted human operator, those complements can remain scarce. Neither outcome is predetermined by the byte cost of a copy.

## 7. When persistent capability becomes a capital-formation claim

Here “capital” is an economic hypothesis about a reusable resource and its services, not personhood, a new national-accounts category or a conclusion about ownership. [R-11](../R-11/README.md#2-what-must-be-kept-separate) separates production cost, asset stock, capital-service input and welfare. That discipline applies whether the reusable resource resides in weights, code or records.

For a proposed experienced system, ask what has changed relative to a feasible alternative. An adaptation may create value by improving quality, reducing correction, shortening execution, supporting a new task or reducing the cost of learning the next task. Merely recalling more facts or exercising broader permission is insufficient. The improvement must survive long enough, and be used often enough, to justify creating and maintaining it.

### A lifecycle cost account

This is a proposed accounting checklist for evaluating the hypothesis, not an empirical estimate:

- **Acquisition:** demonstrations, expert corrections, data rights, cleaning, labeling, scenario construction and compute.
- **Retention:** storage, replay, retraining, memory consolidation, versioning and backup.
- **Use:** inference, retrieval, context assembly, tools, latency and human supervision.
- **Validation:** held-out tasks, safety regressions, human adjudication, audits and repeated testing after updates.
- **Maintenance:** changed rules, interfaces and personnel; conflicting information; rollback and retirement.
- **Transfer:** export, compatibility work, destination calibration, access approval and authorized removal of restricted material.
- **Risk:** expected error and disclosure losses, downtime and the cost of an update that damages an otherwise useful system.

The relevant savings depend on the comparison. Avoiding retraining from random initialization may save substantial **marginal** compute given a pretrained model; it does not erase the original model's production cost. Retaining a cheap adapter may reduce storage but not the cost of serving its base. A fast lookup may be expensive to index; a high-scoring search may delay the user. Human corrections can be an investment, but not every correction succeeds or generalizes. Failed training runs and abandoned memories belong in an adoption decision even if a successful checkpoint is the only artifact released.

A stronger base model can also depreciate yesterday's customization. The experienced version might remain better at a local task, or a new generic model supplied with the same records might overtake it. A market for experience could therefore center on portable datasets, curricula, tests and procedures rather than aged actor identities. That possibility does not refute the authored hypothesis; it identifies a competing location of the productive stock that research must compare.

### Copying changes reproduction cost, not necessarily formation cost

If one expensive learning history can generate many useful copies, the initial investment may be amortized. If copies subsequently diverge, each may require separate validation; combining their changes need not preserve either's benefits. A fork also creates an identification problem: are later gains inherited, newly acquired, or supplied by changing external resources? A meaningful provenance record tracks that distinction.

The human-apprenticeship analogy fits the need for practice, feedback and assessment at a functional level. It breaks where digital copying, replay, resets and centralized training replace individually accumulated embodied experience. It also breaks if a model's improved benchmark behavior does not imply judgment under actual consequences. These differences support research into machine development; they do not license importing human wage, identity, maturity or ownership categories wholesale.

## 8. Observation and the provisional tacit moat

The relevant pathway is not observation alone. It is observation → usable representation or training data → update → retained capability → beneficial action in the target setting. Each arrow can fail. A video may omit goals; a log may omit rejected alternatives; the supervisor's correction may be ambiguous; the task distribution may change; the resulting policy may exploit a regularity that does not survive deployment.

[R-06's primary evidence, including VPT](../R-06/README.md#three-different-demonstrations), establishes why a permanent “machines cannot learn this because people cannot fully verbalize it” defense is too strong. Its observation-to-action evidence is not duplicated here. R-14's contribution is to ask whether the resulting adaptation is retained, generalized and separable from the environment and training resources.

Counterfactual absences are an identification problem rather than an automatic refuge. If no action is taken, a passive observer may lack the evidence needed to distinguish prudence from indifference or an unseen constraint. Additional interventions, explanations or feedback can sometimes distinguish them, but this review finds no basis for assuming complete observability. Consent is a normative and institutional constraint, not proof of technical impossibility. Nor does technical learnability establish that observation is legitimate or that people will continue behaving identically under it.

C-047 therefore remains a conditional claim about erosion and limits. The reviewed evidence is compatible with partial capture of previously unrecorded know-how, substantial residual gaps and shifting boundaries. It supplies no general timeline or secure permanent moat.

## What a test of experiential machine capital would require

The following are **auxiliary research tests**, not additional admitted author claims and not experiments performed for this review. They make the remaining causal burden explicit.

### R14-T1 — Locate the retained change

Start from identical model/system versions and assign different experience streams. Record all changed state: weights, adapters, notes, retrieval indices, code, optimizer state and permissions. After a clean restart, test held-out tasks. Reset one component at a time and transplant retained components to an otherwise fresh instance. This distinguishes effect of history from effects of model upgrades, longer prompts, broader authority and extra attempts.

Two estimands matter. **Total system benefit** includes useful curation of experience. **Additional internally learned capability** compares systems with equivalent authorized information and tools while varying learned parameter state. Equalizing all memories would remove part of the asset when estimating the first, but is informative for the second. Neither comparison alone answers both questions.

### R14-T2 — Separate retention, updating and transfer

Use a predeclared task-by-time matrix: old tasks with still-valid rules; old tasks whose correct answers changed; new instances in the same domain; new domains; and tasks requiring combinations never trained together. Measure both old-task degradation and speed/quality of new acquisition. Introduce long gaps, conflicting updates, task-order changes and scarce feedback. Report per-task distributions and serious failures, not only an average that permits gains on easy tasks to mask damage elsewhere.

### R14-T3 — Test actor specificity against portable information

Compare: a fresh base; fresh base plus raw authorized history; fresh base plus curated history/skills; experienced parameter state with matched information; an exact copy of the experienced system; and a newer base with the same artifacts. Randomize assignment of task instances and assessor order. Count resource use in all arms. If all gains transfer with the notes, that is positive evidence for institutional/digital capital but not for a uniquely embodied actor advantage.

### R14-T4 — Test separability rather than infer it from file structure

Construct consented synthetic sensitive facts interleaved with general skills. Test export of each proposed component, removal or unlearning, adversarial extraction, paraphrases and later retraining. Evaluate retained useful capability and disclosure separately against a never-exposed baseline. A clean answer to a standard query is insufficient; an unsuccessful attack is also not proof of universal secrecy. R-15 then evaluates the applicable rights, and R-13 the enforcement boundary.

### R14-T5 — Price completed useful work over the asset's life

Observe task quality, failures, time, human correction and maintenance through an actual update cycle. Compare investment plus operation against fresh-model/retrieval and periodic-retraining alternatives. Estimate break-even use and sensitivity to demand, obsolescence and supervision. Do not equate training expense with asset value or multiply an original's full development cost by every copy. R-11 supplies measurement boundaries; R-17 supplies the human-pair counterfactual.

### R14-T6 — Distinguish education from examination

Expose one set of otherwise identical persistent systems to a curriculum, another to an equal-compute alternative, and another only to evaluation. Freeze the assessment tasks and prevent answer leakage. Test after restart, after later unrelated learning and in consequentially relevant environments. Passing a simulation immediately after repeated exposure is neither durable learning nor demonstrated real-work transfer. This is the persistence gate for R-16's wind-tunnel proposition.

## 11. Investigated gaps, dependencies and author-only questions

### Gaps left after substantive investigation

- **Lifetime accumulation:** the reviewed studies contain short task sequences, large training phases, bounded simulated tasks or constructed histories. They do not identify years-long economic learning curves for individually persistent general-purpose actors. That is a gap in this reviewed evidence, not proof that no such experiment exists anywhere.
- **Net economic return:** latency, memory, compute and benchmark quality are partially observed; full supervision, governance, integration, opportunity cost and depreciation are not jointly measured for the proposed asset.
- **Novel knowledge versus elicitation:** adaptation can improve the tested response without showing that an entirely new general skill has formed. Controlled synthetic information, held-out combinations and matched-context comparisons are needed.
- **Robust separation:** selected privacy/unlearning experiments do not certify exportability of a work history containing multiple parties' interests. A technical certificate would still not settle legal rights.
- **Distribution shift:** fixed history corpora and offline training do not fully represent an agent changing its own future observations, organizational responses or incentives.
- **Provider continuity:** product descriptions document available mechanisms, but not lifetime portability or retention across unannounced model/runtime changes.
- **Tail outcomes:** repeated average task success cannot alone establish safety for rare consequential failures. R-13 and R-16 must retain that burden.

### Reconciliation required across programs

- **R-13:** external state can retain both good and bad lessons. Durability, provenance and permission enforcement must be evaluated independently. The RLHF correction is shared; R-14 does not revisit the whole MKS reliability architecture.
- **R-15:** retain its four-part separation of technical reproduction, legal permission, operational portability and economic portability. R-14 supplies partial technical evidence but no general separability result or rights allocation.
- **R-16:** [its professional-development and simulation review](../R-16/README.md#post-training-is-real-professional-development-is-a-stronger-claim) should use the actual saved update and post-update retention as prerequisites for professional development. Keep foundation/family training separate from development of one deployed actor. SEAL and Titans are technical precedents with the limits above.
- **R-17:** compare the human–AI pair with fresh models given equivalent authorized information, and distinguish a shared history's informational contribution from learned mutual coordination. A transported adapter is not evidence that the human relationship travels.
- **R-03/R-11:** no rhetorical crossing from “performs work” to legal labor status, or from “valuable history” to a recognized accounting asset. Name the service flow and production boundary.
- **R-06:** keep the observation-to-learning route open without equating partial behavioral imitation with complete access to tacit or counterfactual knowledge.

**Author-only choices:** Does Luke want “experiential machine capital” reserved for parameter-embodied adaptation, or a broader family including curated external state? The latter is economically plausible but would broaden §10's explicitly stronger distinction from notes. Does “persistent actor” designate an identifiable history-bearing system, or a particular model instance/checkpoint? Research can clarify consequences; it cannot choose the author's intended ontology. Neither question requires claiming consciousness, personhood or autonomous rights.

### Search, selection and access decisions

The purposive search followed the admitted questions: continual learning and forgetting; rehearsal and pretraining counterevidence; test-time and self-directed adaptation; memory/skill persistence; memory evaluation; parameter portability; and privacy/separation. Search results were leads. Inclusion required substantive accessible methods/results or official documentation that answered a specific boundary question. This is a critical literature review, not a preregistered systematic review or a census of every 2026 memory product.

Primary conference or journal versions were preferred. SEAL's final conference paper governs over earlier arXiv material. LoCoMo's original appendix governs the original study's sample; the later discrepancy is preserved rather than reconciled by guessing. LongMemEval's OpenReview endpoint displayed a browser-verification page; its public ICLR proceedings PDF supplied the substantive text. Nature's reader URLs included a cookie-related query string but returned the substantive article and methods; no warning or access control was bypassed. A nonexistent TOFU v2 HTML request failed; the metadata confirmed v1, which was then read. EWC's linked 2018 critique/reply pages returned cookie-absence pages; no claim from their unread arguments is used.

VPT was inspected as a lead for observation-to-learning mechanisms; R-06 owns its substantive evidence capsule. This review cross-references that treatment and does not duplicate its results. R-14 owns the AgentPoison memory-poisoning capsule, while R-13 owns action/security enforcement comparisons. Neither review treats unread secondary commentary as evidence. Social-media benchmark claims, vendor leaderboards without a stable common protocol, and private implementation inferences are excluded from the evidentiary conclusions.

Geography is mostly an institutional attribute of research teams, not a population of workers represented by these computational experiments. Developer affiliation can create incentives to emphasize a new method; open code or publication does not remove selection, benchmark and reporting limits. The important comparisons here concern training protocols, data construction, model snapshots, resources and task distributions. None is a representative employer-adoption survey or a randomized estimate of long-run labor productivity.

## 13. Review conclusion

The research changes the question from whether machines can remember to what kind of retained change creates useful future services. Both external artifacts and parameter adaptation can do so. Neither should be dismissed, and neither should be inflated into a general experienced actor merely because the system has a history.

The strongest publication-safe position is conditional: machine experience can produce reusable capabilities, while its stability, transfer, separability and economic return require explicit tests. The public MKS progression is governance rather than demonstrated RLHF; the stronger actor-capital extension remains an empirical program. That conclusion preserves the author's future possibility without treating the desired endpoint as an existing product or an inevitable economic category.
