# What the Map Misses

**An open, inspectable book about abundant machine cognition, writable production functions, and the institutions we build around AI.**

*What the Map Misses* began as an essay series about AI, human capability, and the economics of knowledge work. It is becoming a larger argument about what happens when cognition becomes cheap and abundant at the same moment software becomes cheap and plastic enough to reorganize production around it.

This repository is the public home of that work.

The book will not only be published here. **Its production process will be inspectable here.** The manuscript, research, evidence, AI-assisted workflows, author decisions, corrections, and revision history will accumulate in the open as the argument develops.

The polished reading experience will eventually be generated from the same canonical source maintained in this repository.

## The core idea

AI is producing two transformations at once:

1. **Machine cognition is becoming a new, cheap, abundant factor of production.**
2. **Software is becoming cheap and plastic enough to rebuild the production line around that cognition.**

Either change would be significant on its own. Together they mean AI does not merely add a new input to a fixed production function.

**The production function itself is becoming writable.**

The first generation of AI adoption tends to place new intelligence inside old workflows: the same jobs, handoffs, documents, software, approval chains, and organizational boundaries, only faster.

That is analogous to replacing a steam engine with an electric motor while leaving the factory organized around the old central drive shaft.

The deeper transformation begins when the machinery moves.

The relevant unit of analysis is therefore not the model alone. It is the coupled production system:

```text
human cognition
+ machine cognition
+ deterministic software
+ durable state
+ workflow design
+ institutional authority
+ control rights
→ completed work
→ realized value
```

## Where the argument is going

The project asks several connected questions:

- What remains scarce when codified cognition becomes abundant?
- How is human judgment reproduced if AI absorbs the work through which expertise was historically developed?
- What changes when domain experts can create bespoke software around local problems at dramatically lower cost?
- Does AI make productive polymathy — the Renaissance person — economically viable again?
- What does an organization look like when human judgment, machine cognition, deterministic execution, and software can be continually recombined?
- What happens when useful digital capital becomes cheap enough to create without a conventional market transaction?
- And what institutions should organize machine cognition as it becomes more autonomous, persistent, adaptive, and embedded in consequential work?

The final question connects this project to an earlier line of work I called the **Mendelsohn Kernel Standard (MKS)**: a New Institutional Economics approach to organizing stochastic machine intelligence not by demanding perfection from each actor, but by designing the surrounding institution so bounded error is observable, correctable, and unlikely to propagate into unacceptable outcomes.

A working formulation is:

> **Build the institution before the intelligence can remember.**

Today, much of the continuity that future adaptive machine intelligence may eventually internalize has to be built outside the model: durable state, operating norms, authority boundaries, audit trails, tests, recovery paths, and institutional memory.

That half-step is available now.

## Why this book is being built in public

The method is part of the argument.

This book is being developed with extensive use of frontier AI systems for research, synthesis, historical investigation, criticism, consistency checking, and potentially drafting. I am not interested in hiding that process behind a generic statement that "AI was used."

Instead, the goal is to make the actual division of labor legible.

The repository is intended to preserve, where useful:

- manuscript chapters and revisions;
- source and evidence records;
- historical research packets;
- argument and claim maps;
- contradictions and counterarguments;
- author decisions resolving material conflicts;
- bounded AI research and drafting loops;
- validation and factual checks;
- corrections after publication;
- and the Git history connecting those states.

This creates a form of **intellectual provenance**. Readers should be able to inspect how an important claim developed, what evidence supports it, what challenged it, and how the published version changed.

The repository is an intentional public record, not a mirror of my private notes or working context. Only publication-safe material deliberately brought into the project belongs here.

## Authorship and AI

The central ideas in this project grew out of my writing, economics background, software experiments, institutional work, and sustained attempts to understand what changes when machine cognition enters real production systems.

AI systems are part of the production function of the book itself.

They may perform substantial cognitive work. But I retain responsibility for:

- the thesis;
- substantive conceptual decisions;
- interpretation of conflicting evidence;
- normative judgments;
- what becomes canonical;
- the final argument and tone;
- and publication of the resulting work.

The standard is not that the machine never makes an error.

> **I do not require the machine to be infallible. I design the research and writing process so that it does not have to be.**

That principle is also one of the book's claims about the future of AI-enabled institutions.

## The book as a demonstration

This project is intentionally recursive.

The book argues that abundant machine cognition can make previously uneconomical cognitive work feasible; that durable state and institutional design determine whether intelligence becomes useful; and that production systems can be redesigned around the comparative advantages of humans, models, and deterministic software.

The production of the book is itself a test of those claims.

The working process is expected to look roughly like:

```text
existing authored corpus
→ canonical argument structure
→ contradiction and coherence audit
→ author adjudication
→ historical and empirical research
→ evidence-backed chapter architecture
→ drafting
→ cross-book consistency and factual audit
→ authorial revision
→ versioned release
```

Long-running AI work will be bounded by explicit process specifications, durable repository state, checkpoints, acceptance conditions, and human escalation when a substantive conflict cannot be resolved without an authorial decision.

The AI does not need to remember the entire project in its conversational context. The institution does.

## A few propositions under development

> **AI introduces a new factor of production at the same moment the production function becomes writable.**

> **Machine cognition is the new power source. Plastic software is the distributed motor.**

> **The durable advantage is not access to one model or agent. It is the ability to keep rewriting how value is produced.**

> **The Renaissance person can become the entrepreneur of a liquid production function.**

> **When software creation becomes cheap enough, an application no longer needs a market. It only needs a use case.**

> **Alignment is not merely restriction. It is the design of an environment in which imperfect intelligence can act productively toward human intent.**

> **Build the institution before the intelligence can remember.**

## Editions, corrections, and permanence

The intent is to publish meaningful milestones as versioned releases rather than allowing the web edition to become an untraceable moving target.

A future structure may include thesis, research-complete, review, and published editions, with later corrections preserved as explicit changes rather than silent replacements.

Git gives the project something ordinary books rarely have: a recoverable history of the intellectual object itself.

That history is part of the experiment.
