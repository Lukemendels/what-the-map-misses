# D-002 — Is ownership a robust conclusion or a conditional strategy?

**Status:** PENDING — AUTHOR_DECISION_REQUIRED. The candidate leaves this conclusion conditional.

## 1. Decision ID

D-002. Audit A-09; claims C-009, C-039–C-041, C-045 and C-050.

## 2. Exact crux

Does owning productive machinery remain the one winning position even if the human tacit moat disappears, or does ownership's value depend on ongoing redesign, competition and effective control rights just as other productive positions do?

## 3. Position A — Ownership wins under either human-moat outcome

C-040; S-06, `source/drafts/essay-5-seed-what-i-cant-see.md`, “The resolution”: “If it erodes: the last labor moat drains, and ONLY ownership still pays”; “Build the loom wins in both worlds.” Its closing calls ownership “the one position robust to my being wrong.” This is the explicit conclusion of the earlier five-essay arc, not merely a suggestion that ownership can sometimes help.

## 4. Position B — Value capture remains conditional

C-039/C-041/C-045. S-03, `source/retired/03-portugal-didnt-have-to-lose.md`, “The decision,” distinguishes architect wages from ownership and gives the owner “full exposure to the risk.” S-07, `source/drafts/cognitive-factory-floor.md`, Phase 3 says competition destroys abnormal corporate profits. S-04, `source/drafts/04-the-production-function-is-becoming-writable-v3.md`, “The danger is not only moving too slowly,” denies permanent differentiation from one workflow and locates durable capability in repeated redesign. S-10 §§3–4,7,13, `source/synthesis/2026-09-11-current-author-synthesis.md`, distinguishes design-dependent capture, cheap creation versus valuable services, and unsettled worker/firm control rights. C-009's redesign advantage itself is a forecast requiring evidence.

## 5. Why both cannot survive unchanged

An unconditional “wins in both worlds” prescription is stronger than a conditional account of rents, risk and control. The relevant conflict is the strength of the conclusion, not whether ownership and redesign can coexist. The corpus does not establish that owning cheap reproducible machinery protects returns when imitation spreads, that every owner can keep redesigning, or that architectural contribution brings residual ownership. Nor does it explicitly retract S-06's dominance claim. Substituting “ownership may help” would resolve the problem by changing what the earlier conclusion says.

## 6. If A is chosen

Ownership remains the arc's robust destination, including after cognitive substitution. The book must define the owned asset and establish why relevant rights and returns survive imitation and falling machinery costs, and distinguish dominance of a strategy from guaranteed success for an individual owner. The limits chapter cannot rely on an unearned ownership escape hatch.

## 7. If B is chosen

The book presents ownership as one contingent strategy and governance variable. Its conclusion centers on adaptive productive capacity, purpose, rights and distribution without promising an individually secure destination. The earlier two-world dominance ending must be explicitly revised rather than silently weakened.

## 8. Is a third synthesis available?

Possibly: advocate ownership of an evolving productive system, including relationships and residual control, paired with continuing redesign rather than possession of a static workflow. That could preserve part of the earlier prescription. It requires Luke to define the asset and decide how much robustness is claimed; it is not already a demonstrated resolution. Cheap capital services and durable scarcity rents remain different.

## 9. Evidence that could help

R-05/R-12/R-15: competition, imitation, returns to architectural work, worker bargaining, property rights and control over evolving systems. Evidence can identify conditions under which ownership pays; it cannot choose whether the book recommends ownership as a universal destination or accepts a contingent conclusion.

## 10. Question for Luke

Should ownership remain the book's robust answer in both human-moat scenarios, become a conditional strategy, or be reformulated around ownership and continuous development of a specifically defined productive system?
